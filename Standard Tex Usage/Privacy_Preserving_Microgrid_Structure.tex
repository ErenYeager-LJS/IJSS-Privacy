\begin{abstract}
Distributed secondary control in islanded AC microgrids must coordinate physical regulation with communication constraints and incomplete adversary access. This paper studies a secondary-control architecture equipped with a public/private virtual coordination decomposition and a passive public-history observation model. The analysis is deliberately restricted to a local-before-exit theorem boundary. First, we establish local well-posedness and compact-region comparison properties for the physical closed loop, including regular prescribed-performance coordinates, a finite privacy-residual bound, and actuator feasibility on the selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0$. Second, we construct a non-nominal private realization that produces the same complete public history as a nominal realization under the stated privacy-domain margins. The initial alternative construction and its denominator-valid continuation are treated separately, so the privacy statement remains existence-based and local. The results apply only before the earliest admissibility exit or finite privacy-seed boundary. They do not assert global continuation, all-time invariance, post-seed validity, asymptotic residual decay, deadline recovery, active-power sharing, or a simultaneous composite guarantee.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Islanded AC microgrid, distributed secondary control, public/private coordination, prescribed-performance control, public-history indistinguishability, passive eavesdropper, local-before-exit analysis.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Islanded AC microgrids rely on hierarchical control to coordinate inverter-based units, with the secondary layer compensating deviations left by primary droop control \cite{Guerrero2011,Bidram2012}. Distributed implementations replace a central coordinator with neighbor-to-neighbor information exchange and have been used for secondary voltage regulation and coordination \cite{Lantao2019,Bu2025}. The communication layer is therefore part of the control loop: public coordination states are exchanged among neighboring units, whereas local controller variables and physical measurements may remain private. A useful analysis has to describe both the physical closed loop and the information available to an observer of that communication network.

Communication dependence also exposes coordination data to passive observation. Privacy-preserving distributed-control research spans mechanisms with materially different guarantees; in particular, state-decomposition methods create alternative internal realizations while preserving declared public coordination behavior \cite{SD,SD-MSR,YeCaoChowCai2024}. Such an observation-equivalence objective is distinct from encryption and differential privacy. It is also distinct from cyber-resilience against an adversary that modifies messages. The present passive model addresses only what can be inferred from the declared public history.

Physical regulation and observation equivalence are not interchangeable. A controller can be physically regular on an admissible operating region without preventing multiple private explanations of the same communication record. Conversely, a state decomposition that hides local variables does not by itself establish that the coupled microgrid equations remain well posed, that the prescribed-performance coordinates are regular, or that the implemented inputs remain feasible on the design region. Prescribed-performance transformations provide a systematic way to encode transient-error envelopes in nonlinear control \cite{Bechlioulis2008}; this concept must not be confused with prescribed-time secondary-control results, whose claims concern convergence by a designer-selected time \cite{Bu2025}. The resulting proof problem is coupled, but its physical and observation-equivalence arguments must remain logically separate.

This paper asks whether a distributed secondary controller can retain a rigorous local physical analysis while preventing unique reconstruction of protected virtual coordination data from the complete passive public history. We address this question using a public/private virtual coordination layer, a fixed connected cyber graph, and an explicitly bounded observation model. The controller, plant equations, prescribed-performance construction, and privacy mechanism are held fixed throughout the analysis. Physical estimates are stated on the selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0$ before the first admissibility exit, and the privacy construction stops at the earliest finite-seed or regular-domain boundary.

The paper makes two bounded contributions. First, it establishes a local physical result for the closed loop: local well-posedness, finite component and composite comparison bounds, a finite privacy-residual bound, and actuator feasibility on the selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0$. Second, it establishes a local public-history indistinguishability result: an admissible non-nominal private realization is constructed and continued while the strict privacy-domain conditions remain valid, producing the same complete passive public history as the nominal realization. The two results are reported separately. Deadline recovery, asymptotic residual decay, active-power sharing, forward invariance, global continuation, and a simultaneous composite theorem are outside the final theorem scope.

The contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A local-before-exit analysis of the distributed secondary-control closed loop. On the selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0$, the plant, prescribed-performance coordinates, reconstruction interface, and privacy residual admit finite local bounds, and the composite comparison inequality holds under the stated feasibility conditions.
    \item A local public-history indistinguishability construction under the passive observation model. The initial non-nominal realization and its denominator-valid continuation are established in separate construction stages, and the claim stops at the finite-seed or regular-domain boundary.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:system_model}

\subsection{Islanded Microgrid and Droop Model}

Consider an islanded AC microgrid containing $N$ inverter-based distributed generators (DGs), indexed by $\mathcal V=\{1,\ldots,N\}$. The hierarchical separation between primary droop regulation and secondary coordination follows the standard microgrid-control organization in \cite{Guerrero2011,Bidram2012}. For DG $i$, let $V_i$, $\omega_i$, and $\delta_i$ denote the voltage amplitude, angular frequency, and phase angle, respectively. The voltage and frequency channel states are
\begin{equation}
\bm{x}_i^V=
\begin{bmatrix}x_{i,1}^V & x_{i,2}^V\end{bmatrix}^{\!T}
=\begin{bmatrix}V_i & \dot V_i\end{bmatrix}^{\!T},
\qquad x_i^\omega=\omega_i,
\qquad \dot\delta_i=\omega_i.
\label{eq:physical_states}
\end{equation}
The reduced droop dynamics used in this work are
\begin{align}
\tau_{P_i}\dot\omega_i
&=-(\omega_i-\omega_{\mathrm{ref}})
-k_{P_i}(P_i-P_i^d)-u_i^\omega
+\tau_{P_i}R_i^\omega,
\label{eq:frequency_droop}\\
\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}\ddot V_i
&=-(\tau_{Q_i}+k_{V_i})\dot V_i
-(V_i-V_{\mathrm{ref}})
-k_{Q_i}(Q_i-Q_i^d)-u_i^V
+\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}R_i^V.
\label{eq:voltage_droop}
\end{align}
Here, $\tau_{P_i}$ and $\tau_{Q_i}$ are filter time constants, $k_{P_i}$ and $k_{Q_i}$ are droop coefficients, $k_{V_i}$ is the voltage-loop coefficient, and $P_i^d$ and $Q_i^d$ are droop setpoints. The terms $R_i^\omega$ and $R_i^V$ collect bounded physical or model uncertainty in their respective channels. Privacy residuals are not included in these terms.

The electrical network is represented by $\mathcal G_e=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E_e)$ with electrical neighbor set $\mathcal N_i^e$. Under the adopted lossless inductive specialization, let $B_{ik}$ denote the line susceptance magnitude and define $\delta_{ik}=\delta_i-\delta_k$. The active and reactive powers are
\begin{align}
P_i
&=P_i^L(V_i)+\sum_{k\in\mathcal N_i^e}
V_iV_k|B_{ik}|\sin(\delta_{ik}),
\label{eq:active_power_flow}\\
Q_i
&=Q_i^L(V_i)+V_i^2\sum_{k\in\mathcal N_i^e}|B_{ik}|
-\sum_{k\in\mathcal N_i^e}V_iV_k|B_{ik}|\cos(\delta_{ik}),
\label{eq:reactive_power_flow}
\end{align}
where $P_i^L(V_i)$ and $Q_i^L(V_i)$ are the local voltage-dependent load functions. Conductance terms are not absorbed into $R_i^\omega$ or $R_i^V$.

For later analysis, define the known channel drifts
\begin{align}
F_i^\omega
&=-\frac{(\omega_i-\omega_{\mathrm{ref}})
+k_{P_i}(P_i-P_i^d)}{\tau_{P_i}},
\label{eq:frequency_drift}\\
F_i^V
&=-\frac{(\tau_{Q_i}+k_{V_i})x_{i,2}^V
+(x_{i,1}^V-V_{\mathrm{ref}})
+k_{Q_i}(Q_i-Q_i^d)}{\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}}.
\label{eq:voltage_drift}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:frequency_droop} and \eqref{eq:voltage_droop} can then be written as
\begin{align}
\dot x_{i,1}^V&=x_{i,2}^V,\nonumber\\
\dot x_{i,2}^V&=F_i^V-\frac{u_i^V}{\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}}+R_i^V,
\label{eq:voltage_input_affine}\\
\dot\omega_i&=F_i^\omega-\frac{u_i^\omega}{\tau_{P_i}}+R_i^\omega.
\label{eq:frequency_input_affine}
\end{align}
The privacy-aware coordination layer reaches the plant through the unique interface
\begin{equation}
u_i^V=\widehat c_i^V,
\qquad
u_i^\omega=\widehat c_i^\omega.
\label{eq:plant_interface}
\end{equation}
No public or private coordination state enters \eqref{eq:voltage_input_affine}--\eqref{eq:frequency_input_affine} through another path.

\subsection{Electrical and Cyber Graphs}

The electrical graph $\mathcal G_e$ determines the power-flow coupling in \eqref{eq:active_power_flow}--\eqref{eq:reactive_power_flow}. In line with distributed secondary-control architectures \cite{Lantao2019,Bu2025}, communication is described separately by a fixed, connected, undirected cyber graph $\mathcal G_c=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E_c)$. Its symmetric adjacency weights and Laplacian are
\begin{equation}
a_{ij}=a_{ji}>0\quad\text{if }(i,j)\in\mathcal E_c,
\qquad
L_c=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\sum_j a_{ij}\right)-[a_{ij}],
\label{eq:cyber_graph}
\end{equation}
with $a_{ij}=0$ otherwise. Reference access is encoded by $b_i\geq0$, with at least one pinned DG in each regulated channel. The corresponding pinned matrix is $L_c+\operatorname{diag}(b_i)$. The electrical and cyber neighbor sets, $\mathcal N_i^e$ and $\mathcal N_i^c$, are distinct model objects and need not coincide.

\subsection{Public/Private Coordination Interface}

For each channel $\nu\in\{V,\omega\}$, $p_i^\nu$ denotes the public coordination substate and $q_i^\nu$ denotes the private substate retained by DG $i$. The private weights, residual variables, and locally reconstructed commands are also retained locally. The regular message transmitted by DG $i$ is exactly
\begin{equation}
\bm{m}_i(t)=
\begin{bmatrix}p_i^V(t)&p_i^\omega(t)\end{bmatrix}^{T}.
\label{eq:public_message}
\end{equation}
Thus, raw $V_i$, $\omega_i$, $P_i$, $Q_i$, the nominal coordination state $c_i$, the reconstructed command $\widehat c_i$, $q_i$, privacy residuals, private weights, physical uncertainty, and private controller memory are not regular message components. This ownership convention does not mask the physical state from an adversary that obtains an independent physical-sensor channel; such a channel is outside the declared observation model.

\subsection{Passive Observation Map}

At time $t$, DG $i$ has access to the neighboring message history
\begin{equation}
\{\bm{m}_j(s):j\in\mathcal N_i^c,\ 0\leq s\leq t\}.
\label{eq:neighbor_history}
\end{equation}
The passive eavesdropper observes the complete public history
\begin{align}
\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t]
={}&\{\bm{m}_j(s):j\in\mathcal V,\ 0\leq s\leq t\}
\cup \mathcal H_c[0,t]\nonumber\\
&\cup\{\mathcal G_c,a_{ij},b_i,V_{\mathrm{ref}},
\omega_{\mathrm{ref}},\rho_i^V(\cdot),\rho_i^\omega(\cdot),
T_V,T_\omega,\text{ declared public controller parameters}\}.
\label{eq:adversary_history}
\end{align}
Here, $\mathcal H_c[0,t]$ contains the disclosed topology, timing, and protocol metadata. Public schedules and their parameters are included because they are part of the disclosed protocol, not because a deadline property is asserted. Private substates, private weights, residual and internal privacy variables, private controller memory, raw physical-sensor histories, and locally reconstructed commands are not observed unless separately declared public. The adversary records but does not alter public messages; active manipulation and communication failures are outside this model.

Two admissible private initializations are publicly indistinguishable on a declared interval when they generate the same $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t]$ under the same public references, cyber graph, schedules, and protocol metadata while their protected initial local virtual coordination states differ. This definition concerns non-unique reconstruction from the complete public history. It does not describe differential privacy or cryptographic secrecy.

\subsection{Local-Before-Exit Problem Statement}

The physical problem is to characterize the specified closed loop from an admissible independent initial state while the solution remains in the open regular domain $\mathcal D_{\min}$ and, for compact-dependent estimates, in the selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0\Subset\mathcal D_{\min}$. The requested result is local well-posedness together with the finite component and composite comparison bounds available before the first admissibility exit. The problem does not require exclusion of that exit.

The privacy problem is to determine whether a non-nominal admissible private realization can produce the same complete passive public history as a nominal realization. The construction is restricted to the stated schedule-regular privacy domain and stops at the earliest finite-seed or regular-domain boundary. No conclusion is sought after that stopping boundary. Section~III now formalizes the coordinates, domains, definitions, and active assumptions shared by these two local problems.

\section{Definitions and Active Assumptions}
\label{sec:definitions_assumptions}

\subsection{Independent and Reconstructed Coordinates}

The local solution is formulated on the independent coordinates
\begin{equation}
X=\operatorname{col}_{i\in\mathcal V}\!\left(V_i,\dot V_i,\omega_i,\delta_i,
p_i^V,q_i^V,p_i^\omega,q_i^\omega\right).
\label{eq:independent_coordinates}
\end{equation}
The physical errors, normalized errors, transformed errors, virtual controls,
reconstructed commands, and privacy residuals are algebraic or smooth images of
$(X,t)$ through the specified maps. In particular, $e_{i,0}^V$, $e_{i,0}^\omega$,
$\sigma_i^V$, $\sigma_i^\omega$, $\zeta_i^V$, $\zeta_i^\omega$, $r_i^V$,
and $r_i^\omega$ are reconstructed analysis quantities; they are not additional
independent states. The augmented analysis vector is
therefore interpreted as the image of \eqref{eq:independent_coordinates}, with
its consistency derivatives supplied by the closed-loop equations.

\subsection{Admissible Open Domain and Bootstrap Region}

Let $\mathcal D_{\min}$ denote the genuine open regular domain of the independent
coordinates on which the plant, controller, prescribed-performance maps, and
privacy divisions are defined. It includes strict funnel inequalities
$|\sigma_i^V|<1$ and $|\sigma_i^\omega|<1$, positive performance envelopes,
the regular privacy denominators, and the physical operating domain inherited
from the adopted model. A compact set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_0\Subset\mathcal D_{\min}
\label{eq:bootstrap_region}
\end{equation}
is selected with the admissible initial condition in its interior. The set
$\mathcal K_0$ is a design and estimation region for local bounds; it is not
declared forward invariant. The first-exit time is the earliest time at which
the independent trajectory leaves $\mathcal D_{\min}$ or a separately declared
local design margin ceases to hold.

\subsection{Definition 1: Closed-Loop Local Solution}

\textbf{Definition 1 (closed-loop local solution).}
Given an admissible initial condition in $\mathcal D_{\min}$, a closed-loop local
solution is an absolutely continuous independent trajectory $X(t)$ satisfying
the specified plant, graph, controller, and public/private coordination equations
almost everywhere on a maximal interval $[0,\tau_{\mathrm{ex}})$, where
$\tau_{\mathrm{ex}}$ is the first admissibility-exit time. All reconstructed
coordinates are evaluated from $X(t)$ and remain consistent with their defining
maps on this interval. This definition asserts local well-posedness and a
stopping boundary only; it does not assert continuation beyond
$\tau_{\mathrm{ex}}$.

\subsection{Definition 2: Public-History Indistinguishability}

\textbf{Definition 2 (public-history indistinguishability).}
Two admissible realizations are publicly history-indistinguishable on an interval
$[0,\tau)$ if their complete passive observations satisfy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t]=\mathcal O'_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t],
\qquad 0\leq t<\tau,
\label{eq:definition_public_history}
\end{equation}
including every transmitted public message and every disclosed element of
$\mathcal H_c[0,t]$, while at least one protected private initial quantity
$S_i$ differs from its alternative $S_i'$. The observation is passive and
complete with respect to the declared public payload and metadata. Private
memory, private weights, residuals, locally reconstructed commands, and raw
physical-sensor histories are excluded unless explicitly declared public. The
definition is existence-based and does not assert cryptographic secrecy or
uniqueness failure outside the declared local stopping interval.

\subsection{Assumption 1: Local Physical and Graph Regularity}

\textbf{Assumption 1.} The physical model is defined on the declared
operating region $\Delta$, its channel parameters and positive time constants
are regular, and the load and power-flow maps are locally regular there. The
cyber graph $\mathcal G_c$ is fixed, connected, undirected, and has the declared
pinning needed by the distributed errors; the electrical graph remains a
separate plant object. References and performance envelopes are public and
regular with strictly positive radii. The uncertainty signals $R_i^V$ and
$R_i^\omega$ are measurable and locally essentially bounded on compact subsets
of $\Delta$ with the declared bounds. The initial condition satisfies the
strict funnel feasibility conditions and belongs to the interior of
$\mathcal D_{\min}$. The feasible actuator sets $U_i$ and specified input maps are
locally regular; actuator feasibility on the selected compact bootstrap region
$\mathcal K_0$ is a design
check verified by the local actuator-demand test in Section~IV, not a global
premise of this assumption.

\subsection{Assumption 2: Privacy-Domain Regularity}

\textbf{Assumption 2.} For every affected agent/channel pair, the nominal
initial private split has the declared nonzero margin $\eta_{z,i}^\nu>0$,
the nominal private weights lie strictly inside their declared lower and upper
bounds with margin
$\eta_{w,i}^\nu>0$, and the prescribed privacy schedule satisfies
$\gamma_{\mathrm{priv},i}^\nu(t)\geq\eta_{\gamma,i}^\nu>0$ on the common finite
seed interval $I_s=[0,T_s]$. The margins satisfy the stated feasibility
relations, and the privacy gains and finite local residual budget obey the
declared Privacy Gain Feasibility Condition on the selected compact bootstrap
region. The schedule is regular enough for the finite local residual estimate;
no asymptotic residual conclusion is included here. The adversary is passive
and observes exactly the complete public history in Definition 2.

Assumption 2 does not assert the existence of a non-nominal admissible
alternative, a positive perturbation radius, or a compatible private trajectory;
these are conclusions of the constructive privacy analysis. It also does not
assert denominator validity beyond the initial construction interval. Any
additional denominator validity, continuation, or compatible extension up to
the retained privacy stopping boundary must be established by the later local
continuation argument. Neither assumption asserts global invariance or
continuation.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the local physical analysis]
The selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0$ supplies the finite
constants used in Section~IV; it is not assumed to be forward invariant.
Accordingly, the definitions and assumptions above permit a first
admissibility exit and do not supply a conclusion beyond it.
\end{remark}

With the independent coordinates, stopping boundary, and compact region now
fixed, Section~IV derives the physical estimates available on this local
interval. The privacy construction is treated separately afterward.

\section{Local Physical Analysis}
\label{sec:local_physical_analysis}

\subsection{Prescribed-Performance Coordinates and Specified Controller}

For $\nu\in\{V,\omega\}$, the prescribed-performance envelope is
\begin{equation}
\rho_i^\nu(t)
=\rho_{i,\infty}^\nu+
  (\rho_{i,0}^\nu-\rho_{i,\infty}^\nu)h(t/T_\nu),
\label{eq:ppc_envelope}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h(s)=
\begin{cases}
1-10s^3+15s^4-6s^5,&0\leq s\leq1,\\
0,&s>1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ppc_shape}
\end{equation}
The normalized and transformed coordinates are
\begin{equation}
\sigma_i^\nu=\frac{e_{i,0}^\nu}{\rho_i^\nu},
\qquad
\zeta_i^\nu=\operatorname{atanh}(\sigma_i^\nu),
\qquad
h_i^\nu=\frac{1}{\rho_i^\nu[1-(\sigma_i^\nu)^2]}.
\label{eq:ppc_coordinates}
\end{equation}
They are well defined only in the strict interior $|\sigma_i^\nu|<1$.
For the voltage channel, the retained virtual control and backstepping error are
\begin{align}
\alpha_i^V
&=\sigma_i^V\dot\rho_i^V
-k_1^V\rho_i^V[1-(\sigma_i^V)^2]\zeta_i^V,
\nonumber\\
\chi_i^V&=x_{i,2}^V-\alpha_i^V,
\label{eq:voltage_virtual_control}
\end{align}
whereas the frequency channel uses
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i^\omega
=\sigma_i^\omega\dot\rho_i^\omega
-k_1^\omega\rho_i^\omega[1-(\sigma_i^\omega)^2]\zeta_i^\omega.
\label{eq:frequency_virtual_control}
\end{equation}
The nominal commands and the privacy-aware plant inputs are
\begin{align}
c_i^V
&=\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}\!\left(F_i^V-\dot\alpha_i^V+k_2^V\chi_i^V
+h_i^V\zeta_i^V\right)+k_c^V e_i^V,
\label{eq:local_voltage_command}\\
c_i^\omega
&=\tau_{P_i}(F_i^\omega-\alpha_i^\omega)+k_c^\omega e_i^\omega,
\label{eq:local_frequency_command}\\
u_i^V&=c_i^V+r_i^V,
\qquad
u_i^\omega=c_i^\omega+r_i^\omega.
\label{eq:local_reconstructed_inputs}
\end{align}
Substitution through the unique plant interface gives the transformed
subsystems
\begin{align}
\dot\zeta_i^V
&=-k_1^V\zeta_i^V+h_i^V\chi_i^V,
\nonumber\\
\dot\chi_i^V
&=-k_2^V\chi_i^V-h_i^V\zeta_i^V
-\frac{k_c^V e_i^V+r_i^V}{\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}}+R_i^V,
\label{eq:local_voltage_subsystem}\\
\dot\zeta_i^\omega
&=-k_1^\omega\zeta_i^\omega
+h_i^\omega\!\left(
-\frac{k_c^\omega e_i^\omega+r_i^\omega}{\tau_{P_i}}
+R_i^\omega\right).
\label{eq:local_frequency_subsystem}
\end{align}
These identities retain the privacy residual explicitly and do not absorb it
into the physical uncertainty.

\subsection{Local Well-Posedness and First-Exit Interval}

On $\mathcal D_{\min}$, the load and power-flow maps, graph maps, PPC
transformations, and algebraic controller maps are locally regular. The product
$g_i^\nu z_i^\nu$ in the privacy dynamics is locally Lipschitz in the private
difference, including at $|z_i^\nu|=\gamma_{\mathrm{priv},i}^\nu$.
Together with the measurable, locally essentially bounded uncertainty and
privacy coefficients in Assumptions 1--2, the reduced independent vector field
satisfies the Caratheodory conditions. The local existence and uniqueness
argument therefore supplies a unique absolutely continuous maximal local
solution from every admissible initial condition.

All analysis below is restricted to compact time intervals before
$\tau_{\mathrm{ex}}$, while the independent trajectory remains in
$\mathcal D_{\min}$ and in the selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0$.
On such intervals, all
reconstructed coordinates and compact-dependent constants are finite. This
local result neither excludes a later admissibility exit nor extends the
trajectory beyond it.

\subsection{Command-Rate and Finite Residual Bounds}

For each channel, subtraction and averaging of the specified public/private
dynamics give
\begin{align}
\dot z_i^\nu
&=-[\lambda_{\mathrm{tr},i}^\nu+w_{i,12}^\nu
+w_{i,21}^\nu g_i^\nu]z_i^\nu,
\label{eq:local_z_dynamics}\\
\dot r_i^\nu
&=-\lambda_{\mathrm{tr},i}^\nu r_i^\nu
+\frac{1}{2}(w_{i,12}^\nu-w_{i,21}^\nu g_i^\nu)z_i^\nu
-\dot c_i^\nu.
\label{eq:local_r_dynamics}
\end{align}
The first identity gives a channel-wise exponential bound on $z_i^\nu$.
Differentiating the specified command maps on $\mathcal K_0$ then gives finite
compact-dependent command-rate constants
\begin{equation}
\|\dot{\bm c}^V\|\leq C_c^V(\mathcal K_0),
\qquad
\|\dot{\bm c}^\omega\|\leq C_c^\omega(\mathcal K_0),
\label{eq:local_command_rate_bounds}
\end{equation}
before the first exit while the trajectory remains in $\mathcal K_0$.
Since $r_i^\nu(0)=0$, variation of constants in
\eqref{eq:local_r_dynamics} yields
\begin{align}
r_i^\nu(t)=\int_0^t &e^{-\lambda_{\mathrm{tr},i}^\nu(t-s)}
\bigg[\frac{1}{2}(w_{i,12}^\nu-w_{i,21}^\nu g_i^\nu)z_i^\nu
\nonumber\\[-1mm]
&\hspace{29mm}-\dot c_i^\nu\bigg](s)\,ds.
\label{eq:local_residual_convolution}
\end{align}
The resulting convolution estimate is finite on the same compact local
interval. A bounded command rate supports only this finite convolution estimate;
no residual convergence conclusion follows from it.

\subsection{Voltage and Frequency Component Inequalities}

Consider the specified Lyapunov components
\begin{align}
\mathscr V_V
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_i\!\left[p_\zeta^V(\zeta_i^V)^2
+p_\chi^V(\chi_i^V)^2\right],
\nonumber\\
\mathscr V_\omega
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_i(\zeta_i^\omega)^2,
\nonumber\\
\mathscr V_{\mathrm{priv}}
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,\nu}p_c^\nu
\left[(z_i^\nu)^2+(r_i^\nu)^2\right],
\nonumber\\
\mathscr V_{\mathrm{cl}}
&=\mathscr V_V+\mathscr V_\omega+\mathscr V_{\mathrm{priv}}.
\label{eq:frozen_lyapunov_components}
\end{align}
On $\mathcal K_0$, define the disturbance aggregates
\begin{align}
D_i^V
&=\frac{k_c^V|e_i^V|+|r_i^V|}{\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}}
+\bar R_i^V,
\nonumber\\
D_i^\omega
&=\frac{k_c^\omega|e_i^\omega|+|r_i^\omega|}{\tau_{P_i}}
+\bar R_i^\omega.
\label{eq:component_disturbances}
\end{align}
Applying the stated metric and Young-inequality bounds gives, pointwise on the
selected compact bootstrap region $\mathcal K_0$ and before the first
admissibility exit,
\begin{align}
\dot{\mathscr V}_V
&\leq-a_{Vz}\|\bm\zeta^V\|^2-a_{V\chi}\|\bm\chi^V\|^2
+\sum_i\frac{(p_\chi^V)^2(D_i^V)^2}{2\epsilon_{V2}},
\label{eq:local_voltage_inequality}\\
\dot{\mathscr V}_\omega
&\leq-a_{\omega z}\|\bm\zeta^\omega\|^2
+\sum_i\frac{(\bar h_i^\omega)^2(D_i^\omega)^2}
{2\epsilon_\omega},
\label{eq:local_frequency_inequality}
\end{align}
where $a_{Vz}$, $a_{V\chi}$, and $a_{\omega z}$ are the positive coefficients
fixed by the stated gain and Young-parameter conditions. Under the Privacy Gain
Feasibility Condition, the privacy component satisfies on the same local region
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathscr V}_{\mathrm{priv}}
\leq-a_z\|\bm z\|^2-a_r\|\bm r\|^2
+d_c\|\dot{\bm c}\|^2,
\label{eq:local_privacy_inequality}
\end{equation}
with $a_z>0$, $a_r>0$, and finite $d_c$. This component estimate uses
\eqref{eq:local_command_rate_bounds}, not a residual-decay premise.

\subsection{Bootstrap Actuator/Funnel Feasibility}

Bootstrap feasibility is checked on $\mathcal K_0$ before the composite gain
closure. For the reconstructed commands, let
\begin{equation}
\bar U_i^\nu(\mathcal K_0)
=\sup_{X\in\mathcal K_0}|\widehat c_i^\nu(X)|.
\label{eq:local_actuator_demand}
\end{equation}
The voltage and frequency command maps give the finite bounds
\begin{align}
\bar U_i^V
&\leq\tau_{Q_i}k_{V_i}
(\bar F_i^V+\overline{\dot\alpha}_i^V
+k_2^V\bar\chi_i^V+\bar h_i^V\bar\zeta_i^V)
\nonumber\\
&\quad+|k_c^V|\bar e_i^V+\bar r_i^V,
\label{eq:voltage_actuator_bound}\\
\bar U_i^\omega
&\leq\tau_{P_i}(\bar F_i^\omega+\bar\alpha_i^\omega)
+|k_c^\omega|\bar e_i^\omega+\bar r_i^\omega.
\label{eq:frequency_actuator_bound}
\end{align}
The symbolic design test requires the resulting compact command intervals to be
strictly contained in the declared actuator sets $U_i^\nu$, together with the
specified funnel, gain, and privacy-feasibility inequalities. It certifies
actuator and funnel feasibility only on the selected compact bootstrap region
$\mathcal K_0$ while the trajectory remains there. It does not establish
persistence of those margins after an exit.

\subsection{Composite Local Comparison}

The graph closure uses the identity
\begin{equation}
\bm e=\operatorname{diag}(b_i)\bm e_0+L_c\bm c
+L_c\bm r+\frac{1}{2}L_c\bm z.
\label{eq:local_graph_identity}
\end{equation}
Together with the specified algebraic command maps, it bounds $\bm e$ and $\bm c$
by the physical, residual, and decomposition coordinates without treating the
graph term as an external input. The component inequalities are assembled with
the sufficient composite certificate
\begin{equation}
Q_{\mathrm{cl}}=Q_0-H^T W_DH\succ0,
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{cl}}=\lambda_{\min}(Q_{\mathrm{cl}})>0,
\label{eq:local_composite_certificate}
\end{equation}
where the frequency factor $(\bar h_i^\omega)^2$ occurs exactly once in
$W_D^\omega$. Consequently, on the selected compact bootstrap region
$\mathcal K_0$, before the first
admissibility exit and while the solution remains in $\mathcal D_{\min}$,
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathscr V}_{\mathrm{cl}}
\leq-a_{\mathrm{cl}}\mathscr V_{\mathrm{cl}}+d_R+d_{\mathrm{priv}}(t),
\qquad a_{\mathrm{cl}}>0,
\label{eq:local_composite_inequality}
\end{equation}
and comparison gives
\begin{align}
\mathscr V_{\mathrm{cl}}(t)
\leq{}&e^{-a_{\mathrm{cl}}t}\mathscr V_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)
\nonumber\\
&+\int_0^t e^{-a_{\mathrm{cl}}(t-s)}
[d_R+d_{\mathrm{priv}}(s)]\,ds.
\label{eq:local_comparison_bound}
\end{align}
Here $d_R$ contains the declared physical-uncertainty and compact-region terms,
whereas $d_{\mathrm{priv}}$ is finite by the command-rate and residual estimates
above. Equations
\eqref{eq:local_composite_inequality}--\eqref{eq:local_comparison_bound} are
local comparison statements. They do not exclude a funnel, actuator, physical,
or loss-of-compactness exit and do not provide a conclusion after such an exit.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the local comparison]
The comparison bound describes the physical closed loop only while the stated
open-domain and compact-region conditions hold. It is neither an invariance
certificate nor a prescribed-time recovery result.
\end{remark}

Section~IV has therefore answered only the physical, local-before-exit part of
the problem. Section~V turns to the private-realization construction and its
passive observation equivalence without upgrading the physical result to a
global theorem; the physical and privacy conclusions remain separate
theoretical results.

\section{Local Privacy Construction and Observation Equivalence}
\label{sec:privacy_construction}

\subsection{Privacy Problem Transition}

The physical estimates above show that the specified closed loop is meaningful
on a local interval before the first admissibility exit. The privacy question is
different: it asks whether the complete passive public history determines the
protected private initialization uniquely. The construction in this section is
therefore formulated as a local existence argument under Definition~2. It does
not require the physical comparison bound to become a global continuation
theorem, and it does not claim secrecy against private-memory access,
independent physical sensing, active attacks, or probabilistic disclosure.

The protected quantity for DG $i$ is the initial virtual coordination command
\begin{equation}
S_i=\begin{bmatrix}c_i^V(0)&c_i^\omega(0)\end{bmatrix}^{T}.
\label{eq:protected_initial_command}
\end{equation}
The retained privacy statement is existence-based: for at least one admissible
non-nominal value $S_i'\neq S_i$, the public messages and public metadata in
$\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t]$ are identical to those of the nominal
realization on a common local interval. This is an observation-equivalence
statement, not cryptographic secrecy or differential privacy.

\subsection{Alternative Initialization Construction}

The alternative realization is initialized by keeping the public message fixed
and moving the private component. For the protected channel pair,
\begin{equation}
p_i'(0)=p_i(0),
\qquad
q_i'(0)=2S_i'-p_i(0),
\qquad
S_i'\neq S_i.
\label{eq:alternative_initialization}
\end{equation}
Thus the eavesdropper sees the same initial public payload, while the protected
private initialization differs. The admissibility of \eqref{eq:alternative_initialization}
is not assumed. It follows only for sufficiently small nonzero perturbations
inside the stated privacy-domain margins, so that the alternative split is
nonzero, the private weights remain strictly inside their declared bounds, and
the plant and wrapper variables remain in the relevant local regular domain.

The construction is network compatible rather than single-message based. Once a
non-nominal local perturbation is selected, the physical states evolve through
the same plant interface, the public trajectories are constrained to match the
nominal public trajectories, and the hidden private paths and weights absorb the
remaining degrees of freedom. Consequently, the alternative is a coupled local
realization of the same public protocol, not a post hoc relabeling of one
transmitted signal.

\subsection{Public-History Equivalence Framework}

Public-history equivalence is enforced at the level of Definition~2. For an
interval $[0,\tau)$, the desired equality is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t]
=\mathcal O'_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t],
\qquad 0\leq t<\tau.
\label{eq:local_public_history_equality}
\end{equation}
Because the disclosed graph, references, schedules, timing information, and
declared public controller parameters are the same in the two realizations,
\eqref{eq:local_public_history_equality} reduces to equality of all transmitted
public message histories $p_j^V$ and $p_j^\omega$ for every DG. The private
substates, private weights, residuals, locally reconstructed commands, and raw
physical trajectories may differ, but they are outside the declared passive
observation channel.

The public/private wrapper provides the algebraic degrees of freedom needed to
maintain the same public trajectory. Along an admissible alternative private
path $q_j'{}^\nu(t)$, the public-dynamics matching condition fixes one private
weight, and the private-dynamics equation fixes the other. These forced weights
are legal only while their denominators remain nonzero and the resulting values
stay in the strict declared weight intervals. This is why the privacy statement
is local and tied to a retained stopping boundary.

\subsection{Finite Stopping Boundary}

Let $\tau_{\mathrm{priv}}$ denote the earliest retained privacy stopping event
for the alternative realization. It is the first time, if any, at which one of
the following local regularity requirements fails: the finite seed interval
ends, the alternative independent state exits $\mathcal D_{\min}$, an affected
private split reaches zero, a private weight reaches its declared boundary, a
physical/funnel/input boundary is reached, or another specified regular-domain
singularity is encountered. The local privacy construction is stated only on
\begin{equation}
0\leq t<\tau_{\mathrm{priv}}.
\label{eq:privacy_stopping_interval}
\end{equation}
No conclusion is made at or after $\tau_{\mathrm{priv}}$.

This stopping boundary has two roles. First, it prevents the finite-seed lower
margin of the privacy schedule from being interpreted beyond the interval on
which it is declared. Second, it prevents strict denominator and weight-margin
conditions from being converted into all-time invariance claims. The
construction can be restarted from strict interior times, but only while the
alternative realization remains inside the same local regular domain.

\subsection{Publication-Facing Proof Structure}

The privacy proof in the final results section should be read in the following
order. First, choose a sufficiently small nonzero perturbation of the protected
initial command so that \eqref{eq:alternative_initialization} is admissible on a
common initial interval and produces a genuine $S_i'\neq S_i$. Second, construct
the private path and forced private weights so that every public trajectory
matches the nominal public trajectory on that interval. Third, use the strict
interiority of the denominators, weights, plant variables, funnels, and actuator
inputs to continue the alternative realization until the retained stopping
boundary. Fourth, combine this complete public-history equality with
Definition~2.

This organization supplies the manuscript architecture for the local
public-history indistinguishability result. It does not state that result as a
theorem here, does not assert uniqueness failure for every possible protected
value, and does not extend the observation-equivalence claim beyond the finite
stopping boundary.

\section{Theoretical Results}
\label{sec:theoretical_results}

Section~IV established the local physical estimates for the closed-loop
microgrid, and Section~V established the local privacy construction under the
passive public-history observation model. This section records those two
analyses as separate theoretical results. The first theorem concerns physical
well-posedness and local comparison bounds. The second theorem concerns
existence-based public-history indistinguishability. Neither theorem is used to
derive the other, and the two are not combined into a single composite theorem.

\subsection{Local Physical Result}

\begin{theorem}[Local physical result]
Consider the closed-loop system defined in Sections~II--IV under
Definitions~1--2 and Assumptions~1--2. Let the initial independent state be
admissible in $\mathcal D_{\min}$, and let $\mathcal K_0\Subset
\mathcal D_{\min}$ be the selected compact bootstrap region used for the local
component estimates. Then, before the first admissibility exit, while the
trajectory remains in $\mathcal D_{\min}$ and, where compact-dependent constants
are used, while it remains in $\mathcal K_0$, the closed loop admits a unique
local solution. Along this interval, the prescribed-performance coordinates,
reconstructed commands, and residual coordinates are well defined; the voltage,
frequency, and privacy component inequalities in Section~IV hold; the residual
has the finite local convolution bound described there; the bootstrap actuator
test is valid on $\mathcal K_0$; and the composite comparison bound
\eqref{eq:local_composite_inequality}--\eqref{eq:local_comparison_bound}
holds with constants defined on the selected local region.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textit{Proof sketch:}
Local existence and uniqueness follow from the local regularity argument on the
open admissible domain in Section~IV-B. The command-rate and residual estimates
come from the public/private difference dynamics in Section~IV-C. The voltage,
frequency, and privacy component inequalities are the local estimates collected
in Section~IV-D, and the actuator feasibility condition is the compact-region
test in Section~IV-E. Combining these component estimates through the graph
closure and sufficient composite certificate in Section~IV-F gives
\eqref{eq:local_composite_inequality} and the comparison bound
\eqref{eq:local_comparison_bound}. The argument is stopped at the first
admissibility or compact-region exit.\hfill$\square$

\begin{remark}[Boundary of Theorem 1]
Theorem~1 is a local-before-exit physical result. It is not a global stability
theorem, an invariance theorem, a prescribed-time recovery theorem, a residual
convergence theorem, or an active-power sharing theorem. In particular, the
compact region $\mathcal K_0$ supplies finite constants for the comparison
argument and is not asserted to be forward invariant.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Local Public-History Indistinguishability}

\begin{theorem}[Local public-history indistinguishability]
Consider the passive observation model in Definition~2 and the privacy-domain
regularity conditions in Assumption~2. For a nominal admissible realization in
the stated local privacy domain, there exists at least one admissible
non-nominal private initialization $S_i'\neq S_i$, together with compatible
private paths and private weights, such that the nominal and alternative
realizations generate the same complete passive public history:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t]
=\mathcal O'_{\mathrm{adv}}[0,t],
\label{eq:theorem_public_history_equality}
\end{equation}
for $0\leq t<\tau_{\mathrm{priv}}$, where $\tau_{\mathrm{priv}}$ is the
finite-seed or regular-domain stopping
boundary described in Section~V. The equality is with respect to the declared
public messages, public metadata, public references, schedules, graph data, and
public controller parameters only.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textit{Proof sketch:}
Section~V-B initializes the alternative by keeping the public initial message
fixed while perturbing the protected private initialization. For sufficiently
small nonzero perturbations inside the declared privacy-domain margins, the
alternative split, weights, and local plant/wrapper variables remain admissible
on a common initial interval. Section~V-C then enforces equality of every public
trajectory by fixing the private weights through the public and private wrapper
dynamics. Section~V-D continues the construction only while all strict
denominator, weight, plant, funnel, and input conditions remain inside their
local regular domains. These steps give the complete passive public-history
equality in Definition~2 on $[0,\tau_{\mathrm{priv}})$.\hfill$\square$

\begin{remark}[Boundary of Theorem 2]
Theorem~2 is an existence result for local public-history indistinguishability.
It does not say that every protected initialization has an indistinguishable
alternative, that every perturbation is admissible, or that indistinguishability
continues for all time. It also does not assert cryptographic secrecy,
differential privacy, information-theoretic privacy, or privacy against
observation channels outside Definition~2.
\end{remark}

The two results above complete the theoretical summary supported by the present
local proof chain. Theorem~1 records what can be concluded about the physical
closed loop before admissibility exit. Theorem~2 records the separate local
public-history ambiguity under the passive observation model. Section~VII is
reserved for numerical experiments that test the physical and privacy behaviors
associated with these two results; no simulation design is specified here.
